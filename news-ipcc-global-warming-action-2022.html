UN Scientists Urge Stronger Action to Control Global Warming

The world is heading for a cataclysmic 3C of global warming or worse, scientists from the UN Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) warn. According to the report, goals agreed by countries to limit temperature rises to 1.5C or below 2C cannot be fulfilled unless there are rapid, deep and immediate greenhouse gas emissions cuts in all areas, calling for “substantial reductions” in fossil fuel use to curb emissions and stave off dangerous climate change.

Jim Skea, co-chair of the team behind the report, said the world was “definitely not on track” to limit warming to 1.5C, beyond which the threats of severe floods, droughts, hits to crop yields, rising seas and wildlife die-offs will loom larger. “Unless there are immediate and deep emission reductions across all sectors, limiting warming to 1.5C will be beyond reach,” Mr Skea warned, adding: “We know what to do, we know how to do it, and now it’s up to us to take action.”

As the IPCC released the third part of its sixth assessment report detailing solutions to climate change, UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres described it as a “file of shame” of empty pledges made by countries so far, and accused governments of lying over the actions they were taking. “Climate promises and plans must be turned into reality and action now. It is time to stop burning our planet and start investing in the abundant renewable energy all around us,” he urged. The report draws on 18,000 studies and sources, landing amid an already heated debate over energy supplies and costs prompted by rising oil and gas prices amid Russia’s invasion of Ukraine.

A Crossroads

Hemantha Withanage, chair of Friends of the Earth International, said: “We cannot betray the promise of a 1.5 degree warming threshold. We are at a crossroads. The decisions we make now can secure a livable future.” Dipti Bhatnagar of Friends of the Earth International said there was “no justification for pursuing policies or pathways that allow for an overshoot” of 1.5 degrees, while Meena Raman of Friends of the Earth Malaysia called reliance on speculative carbon removal and geo-engineering technologies “anti-science, and anti-human.”

Based on policies implemented through the end of 2020, the world faces temperature rises of 3.2C by 2100, and warming of 2.8C even if all climate pledges for the next decade are delivered. To give the world an even chance of limiting warming to 1.5C, the report calls for 43 percent cuts in greenhouse gases on 2019 levels by the end of this decade, with emissions peaking between 2020 and 2025. The report also highlights solutions within reach: the cost of solar and electric vehicle batteries has fallen 85 percent in the last decade, wind power has fallen 55 percent in price, and sustainable agriculture, plant-based diets and greener cities can all help close the gap.
