Tunisia Wins Bid to Host Organic World Congress in 2024

Tunisia has been elected to host the 2024 Organic World Congress, an event organized every three years by IFOAM Organics International. Tunisia’s bid to host the event brings Africa to the forefront of global efforts to promote organic agriculture, said UNObio Tunisia, a professional union grouping farmers, processors and distributors of organically-processed agriculture products.

Tunisia collected a total of 60 votes, or 32 percent of all electronic votes cast by IFOAM affiliates, beating four other countries including Denmark. The country is ranked first in the world in terms of area dedicated to organic agriculture and organic olive growing, and is also top of the list of exporters of organic products, according to the Ministry of Agriculture, which also points to an increase of 14 percent in the area devoted to organic farming during 2001 to 2018, 26 percent in stakeholders, 22 percent in quantities exported, and 31 percent in the value of organic exports.

Every three years the Organic World Congress is held in conjunction with a general assembly bringing together thousands of organic farmers, producers, researchers, advocates and policy experts from around the world. The OWC is an opportunity for like-minded stakeholders working towards sustainable agriculture, value chains and consumption to trade knowledge, information on new innovations, and experience, and is seen as a leading event for the global organic sector.

Josephine Atangana, president of AfrONet — an umbrella organization representing organic agriculture actors in Africa working through National Organic Agriculture Movements (NOAMs) — congratulated Tunisia for winning a very competitive bid to host the next OWC in 2024. “We look forward to collaborate and support your team towards hosting the OWC in 2024 and ensure it is a success and generates a higher participation from Africa and our networks across the globe,” Ms Atangana said, adding that AfrONet and NOAMs across Africa are grateful for Tunisia’s efforts to bring the OWC to Africa for the very first time.
