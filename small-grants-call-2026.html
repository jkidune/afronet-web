Call for Proposals

AfrONet Members' Small Grants Programme 2026

29 September 2026 Call published, applications open
20 October 2026 Application deadline
USD 2,000 – 10,000 Grant size per award

Background

The African Organic Network (AfrONet) is a continental membership network established to unite African organic actors and provide coordination and leadership to the organic agriculture sector. AfrONet is registered in Tanzania as a non-profit International Non-Governmental Organization. Its mandate is to develop a unique organic agriculture sector based on ecology, health, fairness and care, contributing to food security, food sovereignty and sustainable development.

AfrONet's Strategic Plan 2023–2028 places emphasis on strong networks, policy and advocacy, knowledge and learning, and a vibrant institution — including interventions on organic trade fairs, regional conferences, exchange and learning, stronger national organic agriculture movements, policy engagement, knowledge services, member needs, gender and youth inclusion, financial systems and organizational capacity.

Purpose of the Small Grants Programme

The AfrONet Members' Small Grants Programme is intended to provide catalytic financial support to eligible AfrONet members to implement focused initiatives that advance AfrONet's mission and strengthen the organic movement in Africa. The programme is designed to support practical, achievable and result-oriented actions that can demonstrate learning, visibility, institutional strengthening and/or wider sector impact.

Objectives

  • Support member-led initiatives that advance organic agriculture and the organic movement at national, regional or continental levels.
  • Strengthen the capacity, visibility, networking and effectiveness of AfrONet members.
  • Promote knowledge generation, exchange, learning and dissemination of good practices in organic agriculture and agroecology.
  • Support policy engagement, advocacy and mainstreaming of organic agriculture in relevant policies, strategies and programmes.
  • Catalyze practical initiatives in organic production, organic standards, certification, markets, value chains and related services.
  • Generate evidence and lessons that can inform future programming, partnerships and resource mobilization.

Priority Areas

Organic agriculture training, knowledge and capacity building
Standards, certification and organic guarantee systems
Policy, advocacy and institutional engagement
Organic markets, trade and value-chain development
Innovative, entrepreneurial and fundable ideas within the agroecology and organic agriculture sector, that could be viably demonstrated through a proposal
Strong networks and organizational/institutional strengthening

Grant Size and Duration

  • Grant size ranges from USD 2,000 to USD 10,000 per successful proposal.
  • Proposed implementation period: 3 to 12 months depending on grant size (USD 2,000 within three months; USD 5,000 within six months; USD 10,000 within twelve months).
  • Applicants should request only the amount necessary and proportionate to the proposed results.
  • Co-financing is encouraged and will be highly considered, but inability to co-finance should not by itself exclude an otherwise eligible member — every AfrONet member is motivated to apply.

Who Can Apply?

Applications are invited from National Organic Agriculture Movements (NOAMs), associations, and networks that meet the following minimum conditions:

  • Be a valid AfrONet member at the time of application and remain compliant with applicable membership requirements.
  • Be a legally registered organization in its country of operation, with proof of registration.
  • Demonstrate a clear mandate and track record relevant to organic agriculture, agroecology and/or the proposed intervention.
  • Have an identifiable office/contact person and verifiable telephone, email and/or website details.
  • Submit the latest available financial and operational reports.
  • Have adequate organizational and financial capacity to manage the proposed grant, or clearly demonstrate how the required capacity will be supported.
  • Not be involved in unresolved fraud, corruption, financial or contractual concerns that would make grant funding inappropriate.

Ineligible Activities and Costs

Activities not related to AfrONet's mandate or the organic sector in general are ineligible, including:

  • Political party campaigning or activities intended primarily to promote partisan political interests.
  • Personal loans, grants to individuals, or onward lending of grant funds.
  • Purchase of land, construction of major permanent infrastructure, or major capital investments unless specifically approved in writing.
  • Fines, penalties, debt repayment, interest on debt, or costs arising from non-compliance.
  • Costs incurred before the grant agreement becomes effective, unless explicitly authorized in writing.

Application Package

Applicants should submit:

  • A proposal for the idea/activity for which the grant is applied.
  • Detailed implementation timeline as an annex to the proposal.
  • Detailed proposed budget as an annex to the proposal.
  • Proof of legal registration in a home country.
  • Proof of valid membership with AfrONet.
  • Latest available financial and operational annual report.
  • Organizational profile and contact details.
  • Any additional document that can potentially support the applicant's proposal.

Enquiries

For clarification questions regarding this Call, applicants may contact the AfrONet Secretariat at kombeladaud@gmail.com (cc info@afronet.bio) or via www.afronet.bio.

Important Notice: AfrONet reserves the right to verify information provided by applicants, request clarification, reject applications that do not meet the requirements for funding, vary the application timeline, or decide not to award grants to organizations whose proposals are found to bear false information. Submission of an application does not by itself create an entitlement to funding. AfrONet is committed to fair, transparent and accountable grant-making; applicants and people involved in the process must disclose any actual or potential conflict of interest, and no applicant or intermediary may offer or request an improper payment, gift or other benefit in connection with an application or award.
