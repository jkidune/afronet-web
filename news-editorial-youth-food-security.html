Editorial: Africa's Youth Hold the Keys to a Food-Secure Future

In this edition of AfrONews, we have celebrated two remarkable success stories. In Tunisia, Haitham Ben Ali sold his car to buy cows, gambled on a native plant called Sulla, and transformed not only his own life but an entire community. In Sierra Leone, Mohamed Kawa lost his job during the pandemic, founded Helzee Farms with nothing but determination, and has since created over 200 jobs while planting more than 20,000 trees.

These are not isolated miracles; they are proof that Africa's youth, often dismissed as jobless and hopeless, are the continent's greatest untapped resource.

But here is the hard truth: for every Haitham or Mohamed, there are thousands of young Africans with the same drive who never get the chance. They lack access to land, credit, training, and markets. They are stifled by policies that favour imported food over local production and outdated systems that shut out new ideas.

To policymakers across Africa, we say this: invest in youth agri-entrepreneurs, create incubation hubs, provide affordable credit, and simplify land access. Support the Haithams and Mohameds before they are forced to sell their last asset just to get started, because the returns are not just economic, they are social and environmental, rippling through communities for generations.

To the youth of Africa, we say: look at Haitham and Mohamed. They started with nothing but a dream and a willingness to fail. The land is waiting, the market is hungry for change, therefore do not wait for permission — go small, learn fast and later scale up.

“The future of Africa's food security is in the hands of young Africans willing to get their hands dirty.”

The future of Africa's food security is not in the hands of foreign donors or distant governments. It is in the hands of young Africans willing to get their hands dirty. The only question is: will we support them?
