Pre-Conference Policy Dialogue Series

6AOC Pre-Conference Policy Dialogue Series

Evidence, solutions and policy pathways for resilient, nutritious and trusted food systems in Africa

Two webinars ahead of the 6th African Organic Conference (1–4 December 2026, Chongwe, Lusaka, Zambia). Outcomes feed into the 6AOC Lusaka Declaration.

Webinar 1 Completed Live now Upcoming

Feeding Africa Sustainably

Scaling Nutritious Food Production through Organic Agriculture and Integrated Agroecological Systems

  • Tuesday 10 November 2026, 3:00 PM EAT
Keynote speaker

Dr David Mulama Amudavi

Founder and Executive Director, Africa Food Systems Transformation Trust (AFSTT), Nairobi, Kenya

A Kenyan agricultural development specialist with more than three decades of experience in agricultural education, research, extension, agroecology and sustainable food systems transformation. He was the founding Executive Director of Biovision Africa Trust (BvAT), which he led for more than 15 years into a respected pan-African institution advancing ecological organic agriculture.

Feeding Africa sustainably is not simply about producing more food; it is about producing diverse, nutritious and safe food while protecting the natural resources that sustain our food systems.

— Dr David Amudavi

From the keynote

Organic agriculture and agroecology must move beyond isolated success stories. We need to scale proven practices, strengthen farmer capacities and create markets that reward sustainable food production.

— Dr David Amudavi

Africa has the knowledge, biodiversity, indigenous seeds and farming traditions needed to build resilient food systems. What we need is greater investment, supportive policies and stronger collaboration to unlock this potential.

— Dr David Amudavi

The goal is not simply more tonnes of staple crops. It is more diverse, nutritious, affordable and safe food — produced in ways that sustain ecosystems and farmer livelihoods.

— Dr David Amudavi

Discussants

Dr Khaoula Mokrani

Regional Knowledge Manager, Knowledge Centre for Organic Agriculture and Agroecology in North Africa (KHNA), Tunisia

Research scientist in agronomic sciences, Vice-Chair of the Network of Organic Agriculture Researchers in Africa (NOARA) and a Board Member of AfrONet.

Mr Kuveya Kenny Chikeya

Secretary, Mpongwe Bulima Organic Cooperative Society, Zambia

A farmer with over 30 years of experience. His cooperative of about 500 smallholders produces certified organic groundnuts and soybeans.

Mr Oyewole Gbadamosi

Coordinator, ECOWAS West African Secretariat of Ecological Organic Agriculture (WASEOA), Nigeria

An agricultural policy and regional programme leader with more than 15 years of experience across West and East Africa.

Key takeaways

  • About 309 million Africans were undernourished in 2025 — roughly 1 in 5.
  • A review of 78 studies found 16% lower food insecurity among adopters of sustainable organic agriculture in sub-Saharan Africa.
  • Kenya's push-pull technology: 75% of assessed farmers reported 3–4× maize yields.
  • Niger's farmer-managed natural regeneration raised sorghum yields by 20–85%.
  • Success must be measured by nutrition, soil health, resilience and farmer income — not yield alone.
